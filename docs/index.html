01

Problem & business context

Airlines receive thousands of tweets a day; most are never read by a person. Automatic sentiment labels (negative / neutral / positive) turn that stream into a measurable signal, and topic labels on complaints (late flight, lost luggage, customer service, …) tell operations teams what to fix. Consumers of the output: social-media care teams (triage negative tweets first), and ops/CX managers (weekly dashboards of complaint drivers per airline).

02

Dataset & sources

Twitter US Airline Sentiment (CrowdFlower / Figure Eight, Feb 2015; via Hugging Face osanseviero/twitter-airline-sentiment): tweets addressed to 6 US airlines, human-labelled by contributors.

  • Task A — sentiment: 3 classes negative / neutral / positive.
  • Task B — complaint topic: for negative tweets, 10 "negative reason" labels; Can't Tell is dropped, leaving 9 classes.
03

Cleaning & preprocessing

  • HTML-entity decoding, lower-casing, curly-quote normalisation.
  • URL removal and @handle removal — handles name the airline, which would otherwise leak the airline into the model.
  • Negation-preserving contractions (can't → can not, n't → not); no stop-word removal, since "not" flips sentiment.
  • Regex tokenisation that keeps words, !/? and emoji as tokens.
  • Exact-duplicate removal after cleaning (retweets/copies): rows dropped, so no duplicate can sit in both train and test.
  • Language detection (langdetect): tweets flagged as non-English — on inspection they were English slang/emoji ("sounds like fun !", "i ❤️ flying"), so the flag is reported but not used as a filter.

The same cleaning/tokenisation is re-implemented in JavaScript so the demo below runs the trained baseline in your browser.

05

Feature engineering

  • Sparse: TF-IDF over word uni- and bi-grams (min_df=2, sublinear tf, 20k features) — bigrams capture "not good", "on hold", "thank you".
  • Dense: TF-IDF → TruncatedSVD (300 components) for gradient boosting, which does not handle 20k sparse columns well.
  • Contextual: frozen all-MiniLM-L6-v2 sentence embeddings (384-d), and full transformer fine-tuning (DistilRoBERTa) with its own sub-word tokeniser.

07

Training & validation

Stratified 70 / 15 / 15 train / validation / test split (seed 42). Hyper-parameters (e.g. logistic-regression C) and the fine-tuning epoch are chosen on validation macro-F1; the test set is touched once. Class-balanced weights in every model so minority classes count.

08

Evaluation metrics

Macro-F1 is the headline metric (each class weighs the same). Also reported: per-class recall, confusion matrices, one-vs-rest ROC-AUC, and 95% bootstrap confidence intervals (1,000 resamples of the test set) on macro-F1.

Limitations

  • Sarcasm & mixed tweets: "great, another 3 hour delay. love it." is scored positive by the bag-of-words baseline.
  • Label noise: crowd labels; most of the confident errors below are short "thanks!" tweets labelled neutral, or detailed complaints labelled neutral.
  • Domain shift: one week of 2015 tweets about US airlines; vocabulary (and Twitter itself) has changed.
  • Multilingual text: the corpus is English; the TF-IDF model has no coverage for other languages and language-ID is unreliable on short texts.
  • Topic labels exist only for negative tweets, and several topics have < 100 examples.
